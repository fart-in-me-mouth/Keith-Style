# Calibrating examples and revision evidence

These excerpts are evidence, not passages to paste into new assignments. Preserve their stylistic lesson without importing their subject matter or factual claims. Original mechanics are retained inside quotations. A quotation may illustrate a useful structure while still containing a mistake that should be corrected in new polished prose.

## Authorship labels

- **Declared original:** Keith explicitly supplied the text as his own past writing or an unedited personal draft. This is user-reported provenance, not forensic verification.
- **User revision of AI:** The comparison shows what Keith chose to change. The change is strong preference evidence; the entire resulting essay is not thereby unaided original writing.
- **Explicit preference:** Keith directly states what he wants. Scope and later instructions still matter.
- **Adopted AI wording:** Approval is evidence of acceptability, weaker evidence of how Keith independently composes.
- **Structural illustration:** An invented pattern used to explain a rule. It is never presented as a real Keith quotation.

## 1. Authentic long-form sentence mechanics

Source for this section: [five blocks explicitly introduced as past writings](https://chatgpt.com/c/6a03a4e3-ca5c-83ea-8c91-9aea88d224ce?messageId=e5c57ea4-6ae4-4308-a3f8-012a6583d974). The first project block uses “we” and may be coauthored; the excerpts below come from the individual reflection, self-assessment, and essays. Independent original-draft provenance is unavailable.

### Explanation stays attached to the claim

**Declared original, self-assessment:**

> I needed improvement in listing the common problems that happen when someone has performance anxiety to further develop others' understanding of the problem or better associate with others who also have performance anxiety.

What matters: a concrete weakness is immediately followed by its purpose and possible effect. Repeating the subject is acceptable. Do not imitate the awkward “associate” construction simply to add imperfection.

### Cause → reader response → purpose

**Declared original, rhetorical analysis:**

> When the reader starts to sympathize with the animals and nature, the author can guide the reader toward their purpose of maintaining wildlife by convincing the audience of animals' and nature's worth by guiding their emotions.

What matters: the sentence keeps developing the psychological mechanism instead of merely naming personification. It repeats *reader/author/audience/nature* rather than chasing elegant synonyms. In new writing, preserve the mechanism without forcing the same density or ambiguous pronoun.

### Concession followed by an actual answer

**Declared original, books essay:**

> Although this may seem like a far-fetched belief that a book about a fictional world could actually influence the actions of others in the real world.

The following sentence begins “However getting ideas into people's minds” and continues the explanation through a concrete historical example.

What matters: he anticipates skepticism and answers the mechanism. This excerpt is grammatically a fragment; that is not a prescription to create fragments in a polished essay. Preserve the objection-and-response relationship, not the accident of punctuation.

### Reflection need not be emotionally detached

**Declared original, reflection:**

> As much as I liked seeing the progress I've made with my performance anxiety I didn't like talking about my feelings because it makes me feel weak as a performer.

What matters: conflicting feelings are stated directly and connected by *because*. There is no elaborate emotional preamble. This is a counterexample to treating one email's “emotionless” request as his universal personality.

## 5. Professional article calibration: formal and accessible

**Explicit preference, current article test, October 29, 2026:**

> personally I feel like I'm not as informal as its trying to make me

Keith then narrowed the problem:

> it did better later in the article but the start of the article seems at least a little too informal when I'm writing professionally I am not informal

This feedback concerns another AI's article produced with the first version of this skill. That article is **not Keith-authored writing**. It is a rejected/partially accepted test output used to identify a register mismatch, not a new authentic style exemplar. His statement supports a strong professional-formality requirement, but not a blanket rejection of the whole article.

### Before: excerpts from the AI-generated test opening

> The Bible is one of the most influential collections of writings in human history, but its history is much more complicated than many people realize. Rather than being written at one time, the Bible developed over many centuries through different authors, religious communities, and historical circumstances.

Later in the same opening:

> Understanding this history requires separating three questions: What do the biblical texts say? What can historians independently establish? And how did ancient religious communities understand their own traditions?
>
> These questions are connected, but they do not always have the same answers.

The mismatch is not slang. It is a generic significance hook, a presumed reader misconception, and spoken tutorial staging. These choices create an informal explanatory manner even with correct grammar. The privacy-essay revision from rhetorical questions to a connected checklist provides related evidence for a more integrated written treatment.

### After: synthetic adaptation for calibration, not Keith's own or approved wording

> The biblical texts developed over many centuries through different authors, religious communities, and historical circumstances. Their relationship to documented history varies: some accounts describe events that historians can independently confirm, while others concern events for which little or no direct evidence survives. Similarities with older writings from neighboring civilizations also help historians examine the development of biblical traditions.
>
> Christianity began as a movement within Judaism and spread throughout the Roman Empire, gradually developing the institutions, beliefs, and collections of scripture associated with Christian churches today. Examining this history requires distinguishing the claims made in the texts, the events historians can independently establish, and the ways ancient religious communities understood their traditions.

This is a style-only adaptation of the supplied article's content, not an independent historical fact-check. The lesson is substantive opening → connected explanation → precise scope, not a fixed two-paragraph template or vocabulary to reuse. Relevant citations must remain attached to their claims when adapting a sourced draft.

### Preserve what already works later

**AI-generated test passage, a calibration candidate rather than Keith-authored evidence:**

> A text can be copied very faithfully while still containing an account that historians cannot independently confirm. Likewise, a text can contain copying errors while describing genuine historical events.

This clearly develops a real distinction in ordinary language. It does not need longer words, an extra transition, or a more elaborate sentence merely to sound professional. Keith did not individually endorse this sentence; its inclusion illustrates why his general preference for later sections should lead to local revision rather than wholesale stiffening.

## 6. How to use the examples

For an email, consult the professional guidance rather than copying an argumentative paragraph's force. For an analytical essay, preserve the mechanism-explaining clauses rather than copying its factual claims. For a light edit, the user's current draft is usually the strongest local exemplar. New, relevant user revisions should refine this guide; they should not be overridden to preserve an old profile.
